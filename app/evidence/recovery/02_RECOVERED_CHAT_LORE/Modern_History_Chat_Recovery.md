## Long-term history, First Greek Naval War, and presidential succession

- `CHAT-RECAP` After a 1930s civil war and collapse of the old absolute monarchy, the 1946 Constitution created a republican executive/legislature while preserving a largely ceremonial hereditary Crown. **Civil-war dates conflict across descriptions:** a 1931–1939 timeline exists, but other descriptions refer to 1943–1946 or a general 1930s collapse. See the original constitutional outline for wording.
- `CHAT-RECAP` Presidency/administrations: 1946 Hartmann (Liberal Independent); 1952 and 1956 Mpik (Conservative); 1960 Anatoli; 1964 Drakos; 1968 Gabris; 1972 Hartmann (the 1960–72 entries described as Hartmann Party); 1976–1980 Conservative administration; 1980–1988 Hartmann Party administrations; by early 1992 Adrien Strevo of the reform bloc/RRP. **These are party/leader succession notes, not a verified table of exact terms and full given names.** Discussed again in a 2025-12-12 master retrospective and a 2026-10-18 summary.
- `CHAT-RECAP` First Greek Naval War, sometime in the **1970s**, originated in disputed Greek islands / Greek-Reunion maritime tensions. The Reunion navy destroyed or neutralized **over 75% of Greece's fleet**. Exact year, battle list, casualty figures, treaty, and commanders were not recovered. Must not invent them. This is DISTINCT from the Soviet-backed war in 1992.
- `CHAT-RECAP` Reunion undertook covert or limited overt anti-communist/anti-junta operations relating to Greece during the 1970s. Relationship to the named First Greek Naval War remains unclear.
- `CHAT-RECAP` 1977 **National Security & Subversive Activities Act** widened powers against hostile-power propaganda, political subversion, or political violence. Exact statutory terms are not recovered here. Source: earlier historical retrospective, not verified bill text.
- `CHAT-RECAP` 1980s industrialization and military-industrial buildup, while expanding Reunion influence in the Adriatic. Not yet a dated event-by-event chronology.
- `CHAT-RECAP` By 1991 Reunion occupied or seized portions of **coastal Dalmatia** amid Yugoslavia's breakup, setting up prolonged dispute with Croatia. Extent and precise operation chronology not recovered.

## Greek-Soviet war, March 1992

- `USER-CONFIRMED/CHAT-RECAP` Reunion backed nationalist forces during a civil struggle in Greece; conflict involved the Soviet-backed People's Republic of Greece (PRG). Reunion's initial offensive gained ground, then Soviet VDV, armor, air support, electronic warfare, artillery and layered SAM/radar superiority reversed momentum. Reunion's slender air force withdrew after heavy attrition; formations fell back toward the **Evino/Evinos River Line**. Direct Greek/Soviet intervention then grew into a war against Reunion.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` Greece declared war; Reunion mobilized armed forces, and the Soviet Black Sea Fleet moved. Reunion ordered its navy to deploy. This decision was recalled from a 2025-06-18 chat. The precise original date of each declaration remains to verify.
- `CHAT-RECAP` Canon battles include Sklavena, Monastiraki, Kallithea (one recovered account quotes ~152 Reunion, ~225 Greek, ~275 civilian casualties at Kallithea), Kryoneri (~220 Reunion, 8 missing), Paliostani (~270 Reunion). Exact day assignments for these engagements are not all secure in recovered chat notes.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **9 March:** Magoula, reported losses ~98 Reunion / ~162 Soviet.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **17 March:** First and Second Evinos; recalled combined ~544 Reunion / ~1,732 Soviet losses. Confirm exact meaning of "losses" (KIA vs all casualties) before using figures in military analytics.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **17–18 March:** Zelenogorsk, recalled ~153 Reunion / ~542 enemy losses.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **19 March:** Rogovo and Pogorevka fighting.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **20 March:** Shakhovka, including failed officer assassination attempt.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **21 March:** Stary Sober and Operation **Ρολόι μηδέν** ("Clock Zero"). The special operation around Thessaloniki neutralized two radar installations and seven SAM/missile-defense sites, killed a Greek general and a Soviet officer, and captured a senior Soviet VDV commander. One prior recap reports ~70 enemy killed, one Reunion operative killed and eight wounded, but check the original scenario before locking that casualty count.
- `CHAT-RECAP` Named formations include **3rd Marine Division**, **Inferno Division** (associated with Henry Steiner), various army divisions, RMC/RIA and Praetorian units. Places occurring in chat accounts include Evino River, Evino village, Mountain Road 12, Broken Highway, Karvounis port, Old Greek Airfield, Hill 214, Olive Groves and Field Hospital Theta. Some of these names came from an AI Dungeon setup/suggested story cards and are **not automatically canon** until independently corroborated.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **21 March:** approximately 15,000 Reunion personnel trapped in the Evinos Pocket; communications degraded to carrier pigeons in an earlier phase. A separate Marine Pocket existed, holding more coherently.
- `CHAT-RECAP` **Operation Iron Vein** eventually coordinated extraction/relief involving army, marines, special forces and navy. Evinos return: ~8,000 fit personnel, ~4,000 wounded, ~3,000 dead (and 71 earlier missing according to a previous account). Marine Pocket: ~4,500 survivors from an earlier trapped force of 7,000+. These totals may reflect different reporting dates or categories; verify before summing.
- `PROPOSAL` Before Iron Vein, alternative operations were **Iron Mist** (submarine/wounded/command extraction), **Burning Gate** (deception), **Crimson Bridge** (ground breakout), **Frost Bell** (surrender trap). In some earlier discussions a force of ~100 untrained rangers and 900–1,400 auxiliaries with scant armor/artillery was proposed. Jamming reportedly unavailable before 3 April. **These were options, not automatically executed.**
- `CHAT-RECAP` 21 March, **Admiral Caldus Rhéon** mortally wounded at an emergency leadership meeting in the Zo Manor/bunker in a suspected Soviet GRU attack; one recap lists death around 22:43. He died that night. The GRU attribution is an allegation rather than conclusively established fact.
- `CHAT-RECAP` **24 March Treaty of Bern** ended direct Soviet-Reunion hostilities. Reunion troop withdrawal from Greece within 14 days; Soviet reduction of central-Greek forward radar/SAM deployments by 30% within 21 days; six-year **$425M** equivalent resource-based payment (crystals/rare earths/gems, obligations begin around July 1992); three Reunion missile vessels dry-docked for 12 months; five merchant submarines subject to civilian-use inspections; Evinos humanitarian corridor for 90 days. **Obligation's payer/beneficiary wording needs exact treaty-source verification.** Talks involved Soviet Premier Somov Antonovich, President Strevo, Gaudé and Belanov in prior summaries. Neither side declared an outright victor.
- `CHAT-RECAP` 3 April Parliament reconstruction legislation reportedly included ~$220M compensation for affected families, Hartmann reconstruction powers, and official Rhéon mourning. Verify exact titles.
- `PROPOSAL` Soviet "Operation Iron Guillotine" GRU infiltration and an April amphibious invasion of Reunion was discussed as a possible retaliation scenario, not proof of an invasion having occurred.

## Political upheaval and 1992 election

- `USER-CONFIRMED/CHAT-RECAP` **6 April** Red Ember Front (REF) assaulted Parliament around noon, involving small arms, snipers, and explosive/grenade/mortar attacks. Initial deaths include **13 parliamentary security, 14 police, 7 army, 2 MPs = 36**. Later fights reportedly cost another 26 police and 9 army lives, alongside insurgent/civilian losses. A recap puts about 80 insurgents killed at Parliament, but exact aggregation is uncertain.
- `CHAT-RECAP` ~200 reservists defected around Steinberg and proclaimed the **Free Republic of Steinberg**; insurgents also seized a broadcast station, targeted a substation, and sabotaged transport around Lucania. Some battle accounts list **Steinberg attack: 112 Reunion casualties, 171 terrorists dead, two LAVs/a Bradley involved**, and **Police Raid on Torrental: 53 police casualties and 81 insurgents dead**. Need reconcile whether these are different scenes/operations or overlapping accounting.
- `CHAT-RECAP` President Adrien Strevo declared ~90 days of martial law and reorganized security. **RNIA Reorganization Act 1992** reformed/replaced intelligence functions of RIA/GRI and provided presidential appointment of RNIA director with **3/5 parliamentary confirmation**; RIA/National Guard role also changed. Alleged Soviet backing of REF remains contested.
- `CHAT-RECAP` Strevo's stroke and resignation triggered a 1992 presidential race involving **Dax Donahue** and **Ezra Hartmann**. Recalled first round Donahue 42%, Hartmann 25%; **5 July runoff Donahue 59%, Hartmann 41%**. Verify turnout and exact first-round share of other candidates.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **Darius Younger** is the CANON Vice President. "Jack Younger" in the October baseline and any other first name is superseded.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` Finance Minister **Dr. Amalia Rehn** died in a car accident **a couple days after her appointment**; **Emilio Curro** appointed as her successor. Exact calendar dates of appointment/accident/succession remain **unknown**. Earlier files may not reflect this.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **Current King Zo III**. The same conversation also identifies his **young heir as Zo III**. Preserve both verbatim; do not invent a correction to Zo IV. Whether the heir's regnal name is an intentional duplicate, prospective title, or different naming convention needs a user decision.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` Parliament grew to **400 seats** to give **Dalmatia equal representation with the three core islands**. This exceeds the 1946 constitutional size limits, so is **unconstitutional but ignored/tolerated**. October 1992 400-seat breakdown authoritative: RRP 118, LUP 95, RCA 82, NSF 64, Others/regionals 41. An older 320-seat alternative must not silently override it.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **3 October, 06:00:** Donahue dissolved/suspended the Assembly for three months, citing legislative paralysis, with plans for negotiations before the period ended. An earlier recap treats this presidential action as constitutionally authorized, which is a *separate legal question from the 400-seat expansion*; assess under the source constitution and laws.

## Dalmatian/Croatian conflict, Bosnia, and the 1992 peace

- `USER-CONFIRMED/CHAT-RECAP` **7 April, Vrpolje:** losses reported around 40 Reunion / 80 opposing militia; **Polača:** ~35 / 48; **Cetina:** ~206 / 416. These are recovered casualty estimates, not clearly divided KIA/WIA.
- `CHAT-RECAP` UDF was a Croatian-backed nationalist militia umbrella. Smuggled anti-tank weapons, pro-Croatian agitation, and Croatian army concentration worsened tensions. One 11 August RNIO briefing put Reunion 3rd Infantry elements at ~3,500 and 2nd Armored Angels at ~5,000; UDF 1,500–3,000, and T-72 sightings. These are local-deployment figures, not entire division strengths. An earlier incident involved a Lynx helicopter shot down.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` **10 September Croatian offensive** near Zadar: Reunion front pierced and positions split. Estimated **560 killed, 1,330 wounded, 260 missing, 370 captured = 2,520 personnel**; loss of **12 AUF 155/39 guns, three AMX-13/90, one EC-130Q and four Mirages**. Reports describe 84 thirty-man platoon equivalents lost. Croatian/UDF losses in a separate account included 18 Giatsint-B, 18 Grad, four T-80 and a MiG-19; these are claims to verify against battle logs, not automatically confirmed as identical battle accounting.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` Croatia settlement: **12-year (1992–2004) non-belligerency pact**, Reunion pulls out of ceded lands, transit and no-third-party-base guarantees, no forward artillery, breach clauses. **Croatian territory runs to just north of Šibenik, which is a DMZ**. Earlier descriptions ending north of Split or treating Zadar–Split differently are superseded. Check exact treaty instrument for legal border and sovereign administration wording.
- `CHAT-RECAP` **Ploče Bosnian militant/hostage crisis** (around 13 September, exact phase dates debated): militants claimed or invoked UN status, seized police/city sites and hostages; Reunion assault/negotiation, Huey shot down; ultimately a UN corridor/ceasefire permitted militants to leave, Reunion held Ploče, and a wider massacre was avoided. Casualty reports CONFLICT: one recap says ~23 Reunion dead / 30 wounded / ~75 Bosnian dead / 120 militants and ~80 hostages; another says **54 Reunion losses**, ~85 Bosnian losses, bridges destroyed, captured personnel killed. Do not merge numbers without matching phase/date/category.
- `CHAT-RECAP` Serbia knew some of Reunion weapons/smuggling talks, negotiated intelligence/security arrangements; Bosnia retained incriminating material; U.S. had financial/FMS oversight leverage. Verify exact commitments and chronology.
- `CHAT-RECAP` **15 December 1992** Croatian MiG-23/Zadar/Split air/artillery clash described in one later battle record, with Reunion Mirages and Croatian fighters downed. This may be a separate event, later development or re-dated retelling of 10 September events. **Keep both until adjudicated**.

## October 1993 Greece–Turkey Altis War

- `CHAT-RECAP` The **1993** conflict is separate from the Reunion–Soviet Greek War of March 1992. Turkey challenged alleged Greek militarization of the Aegean island **Altis**. The originating crisis was developed in older discussions under an October 1992 baseline, but the explicitly dated Altis news document reads **11 October 1993**. Treat year mixing as timeline discrepancy, not grounds to erase the event.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` Turkish missile strikes against radar/military targets followed by a mixed helicopter/sea assault near airstrip and radar heights. Greece attempted reinforcement and localized counterattack; both sides avoided a mainland strike/general naval battle in the confirmed scenario.
- `CHAT-RECAP` By 11 October 1993, after five days fighting, Turkey retained the airport terminal and immediate perimeter but the runway was cratered/unusable; Greece held or retook dominant radar ridge; radar installation destroyed. Earlier estimated Turkish force ~160 fell to ~71 effective, but exact scenario counts must be traced. The Library Variant `Master Political V2(2).txt` provides a much more detailed RBD account.
- `USER-CONFIRMED` Day 6: Greek G3 battalion escalates locally, Alpha element wiped out, Charlie continues holding airport, HQ and remnants of Alpha hold terminal; Turkish airdropped M113 plus two infantry squads was destroyed and most personnel killed; Echo subsequently cleared Greek opposition and planned a radar-hill push. Distinguish the planned push from any result explicitly resolved later.
