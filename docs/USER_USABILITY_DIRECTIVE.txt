# RNAS: USABILITY, INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE, AND WORKFLOW REFINEMENT

You are continuing the existing Reunion National Administration System.

The application has made progress importing historical canon and establishing its database. However, the current interface has become overly cluttered, technical, and cumbersome.

**Your task is to turn the existing application into a coherent, usable national administration system.**

Do not rebuild the application from scratch. Do not discard historical records, existing data, or working features.

## 1. Preserve established economic canon

**Do not change the existing national budget, defense allocation, treasury figures, or other financial values as part of this task.**

The currently reviewed and accepted budget framework must remain as assigned.

Do not reintroduce earlier economic figures because an older source disagrees.

Preserve historical revisions and source evidence without silently replacing current canon.

### Historical date correction

Some Altis events are still dated October 1993 because 1993 was a mistype in previous roleplay records.

Flag and correct these records according to the user's established intended chronology. Where the corrected date is not sufficiently established, present it for review.

Preserve the original source wording and audit history.

Do not apply a blanket date replacement to unrelated historical events.

## 2. Information architecture: reduce clutter

RNAS must support a large amount of historical information without displaying everything simultaneously.

Organize the interface into three levels:

### National overview

Display essential information for managing Reunion:

- Current government and leadership.
- Parliamentary composition.
- Military readiness and major deployments.
- Treasury summary.
- Active conflicts and diplomatic situations.
- Major economic projects.
- Recent historical events.
- Pending decisions and warnings.

Keep this level concise, visually organized, and useful.

### Department workspaces

Each major subsystem should have a dedicated, purpose-built interface.

Government should look like a government roster.

Parliament should look like a legislative workspace.

Military should look like a military command and recordkeeping system.

Treasury should look like a financial administration dashboard.

Historical Archive should look like a searchable historical library.

Do not use the same generic assertion-card layout for every subsystem.

### Evidence and technical records

Original sources, import details, raw JSON, evidence tiers, source line numbers, and audit trails should be accessible through an expandable evidence panel or dedicated historical record view.

Keep this information available, but do not let it dominate ordinary pages.

Use progressive disclosure: show important information first, additional details when requested.

## 3. Redesign the Cabinet interface

The current government interface displays labels such as `Baseline minister`, which are internal data classifications, not useful public-facing job titles.

Replace these labels with the actual offices.

For example:

**Ministry of Defense**
General Marcus Thaler
Minister of Defense

**Ministry of Education and Culture**
Mira Linde
Minister of Education and Culture

Show the President and Vice President in their proper leadership positions.

Use distinct layouts for:

- President and Vice President.
- Cabinet ministers.
- Deputy ministers.
- Vacant offices.
- Government institutions.

Provide a cabinet roster with photographs or portraits when supplied, but do not invent appearances.

Each officeholder should be clickable to open a profile with:

- Full name.
- Office and ministry.
- Appointment date, if known.
- Political affiliation, if known.
- Term history.
- Responsibilities.
- Important decisions.
- Linked legislation and national events.
- Source evidence.

Use readable labels and titles throughout the interface.

**Internal field names such as `baseline_minister`, `existence_in_canon`, and `current_disposition` should never be displayed as ordinary page headings.**

Keep source status and historical uncertainty visible where relevant, without making the page look like a database debugger.

Maintain the confirmed correction identifying Darius Younger as Vice President rather than allowing superseded Jack Younger records to appear as current canon.

## 4. Fix disconnected workflows

The application must be organized around what the user wants to accomplish, not around the order in which database tables were created.

Every major action should be possible from the section where a reasonable user would expect it.

### Military battles and casualties

The current casualty-report workflow requires selecting a battle that already exists.

This is inadequate.

Implement a complete **New Battle / After-Action Report** workflow.

The user must be able to:

1. Open the Battles section.
2. Click New Battle or New After-Action Report.
3. Create a battle or select an existing battle.
4. Enter the name, date, location, belligerents, and participating units.
5. Describe the engagement in ordinary English.
6. Enter casualties when known.
7. Enter equipment losses and operational outcomes.
8. Leave unknown fields empty or explicitly unknown.
9. Review the resulting record.
10. Save it and update relevant military records.

The system must create a new battle and its associated casualty reports together when necessary.

Do not require users to visit unrelated pages to create prerequisite records.

Maintain distinctions between KIA, WIA, MIA, POW, and unspecified casualties.

Use validated database transactions.

### Apply the same principle throughout RNAS

Examples:

- Cabinet: Appoint Minister should let the user find or create the relevant person.
- Parliament: Record Vote should let the user select or create the relevant bill.
- Military: Record Deployment should let the user find or create the relevant formation.
- Treasury: New Expenditure should let the user select or create its relevant program.
- Diplomacy: New Treaty should allow the user to establish missing participating-state records.
- Archive: New Historical Event should support creating related entities without abandoning the event.

Provide inline creation dialogs or guided workflows where appropriate.

Never create fictional historical facts automatically to fill these gaps.

## 5. Report-first interaction

The user prefers to describe events in ordinary language rather than manually maintain every subsystem.

Prioritize a convenient National Report Center.

The user should be able to submit a report such as:

"On October 8, a battle broke out near the Croatian frontier. The 4th Infantry Brigade engaged enemy positions, suffered 32 killed and 114 wounded, and withdrew after six hours."

The system should preserve the original report, identify the engagement, propose a new battle record, resolve the brigade against the existing OOB, prepare casualty records, and ask for approval.

Do not require the user to create a battle, then manually create participants, then separately enter casualties and historical events.

**The primary workflow is: Write Report → Review → Approve.**

The example above is illustrative, not Reunion canon.

AI-assisted extraction can be implemented through the optional provider layer, but the basic structured report workflow must function even without AI.

## 6. Page layout and visual hierarchy

Use a consistent design system, but different layouts for different tasks.

Examples:

**Cabinet:** Leadership section and compact minister roster.

**Parliament:** Visual chamber, party composition, legislative register, current bills, voting actions.

**Armed Forces:** OOB hierarchy, readiness overview, deployments, unit records, battles.

**Treasury:** Financial metrics, budget charts, allocation tables, projects.

**Historical Archive:** Timeline, searchable events, linked entities, source evidence.

**Canon Review:** Conflict inbox, missing-data inbox, proposed-change queue.

Avoid large numbers of nearly identical cards.

Prefer compact tables for extensive inventories, clear charts for statistics, hierarchical trees for organizations, and timelines for events.

Provide search and filtering within major sections.

Keep tables and controls responsive on mobile.

Do not use excessive badges for ordinary metadata.

## 7. Visual identity

Preserve a serious, professional design influenced by Reunion's Mediterranean and European institutional character.

Preferred palette:

- Deep institutional navy.
- Muted green.
- Warm limestone and ivory.
- Restrained brass accents.

Use elegant serif headings with modern, readable body typography.

The application should resemble a polished national administration system with some of the clarity of a grand-strategy interface.

Avoid excessive ornamentation, large decorative panels, generic corporate dashboard aesthetics, or overwhelming statistical displays.

Prioritize clarity and efficiency.

## 8. More useful actions, fewer clicks

Each department should have one prominent primary action appropriate to the page.

Examples:

- Cabinet: Appoint Minister.
- Parliament: Introduce Bill.
- Battles: New Battle.
- Armed Forces: New Formation or Record Deployment.
- Treasury: Record Transaction.
- Diplomacy: Record Diplomatic Event.
- Archive: Add Historical Record.
- National Reports: Submit Report.

Place secondary actions in menus or detail views.

Users should not have to understand the underlying database relationships to perform routine tasks.

## 9. Historical integrity and approvals

No UI redesign or workflow change may silently alter established canon.

Preserve:

- Original historical sources.
- Effective dates.
- Canon approval.
- Proposed versus completed actions.
- Prior officeholders.
- Military unit history.
- Parliamentary votes.
- Financial records.
- Audit history.

All modifications must pass through the existing validation and approval systems.

## 10. Implementation order

Inspect the current UI, components, and database services before making changes.

Implement in manageable steps:

**Step 1:** Improve shared navigation, typography, spacing, and information density.

**Step 2:** Redesign the Cabinet into a real government roster, removing exposed technical labels.

**Step 3:** Repair the Battles and Casualty Reports workflow, including the ability to create a battle and report together.

**Step 4:** Improve the National Archive so extensive records are searchable and readable rather than presented as endless cards.

**Step 5:** Improve Parliament and Treasury views without changing their established data.

**Step 6:** Establish the foundation for a central report-first submission and approval workflow.

Complete and test each step before moving on.

Do not attempt all six steps if the development session cannot support them.

## 11. Acceptance tests

Verify that:

- The Cabinet displays proper ministry and office titles.
- Historical officeholders are not confused with current ones.
- Superseded records do not appear as current canon.
- The user can create a new battle directly from the Battles page.
- The user can create a new battle together with a casualty report.
- Missing or unknown casualty information is supported.
- Treasury figures remain unchanged.
- The 1993 Altis dating issue remains visible for correction until properly resolved.
- The underlying historical evidence remains accessible.
- New records persist after application restart.
- Existing features and approved canon remain intact.

Update implementation status and session handoff documentation.

Continue the existing project. Do not restart it.

**Success means the user can navigate Reunion's government, military, and economy naturally without needing to understand the internal database.**
