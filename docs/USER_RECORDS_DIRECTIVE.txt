# RNAS DEVELOPMENT DIRECTIVE
## Usability, Visual Redesign, and Unified Record Management

Continue developing the existing Reunion National Administration System.

The database architecture audit (Step 2) has already been completed. Do not restart that audit unless you encounter a specific new structural problem.

We are intentionally reducing RNAS's scope.

**RNAS is now primarily a comprehensive national records system, historical archive, and administrative database.**

Its priority is accurate data, excellent organization, easy record creation, readable historical information, and a polished interface.

Simulation features are not the current development priority.

Do not rebuild the project from scratch.

---

# PHASE 1: FREEZE SIMULATION EXPANSION

Stop expanding:

- Autonomous political simulation.
- Geopolitical crisis simulation.
- Military combat simulation.
- Automatic national decision-making.
- AI-driven natural-language extraction as the primary interface.

Do not delete functioning code or corrupt existing work.

Preserve it for possible future development.

The current priority is reliable administration and records management.

---

# PHASE 2: UNIFIED RECORD CREATION

Create a central **New Record** interface accessible prominently from the RNAS homepage and persistent navigation.

The user should not need to search through numerous departmental pages to find where to create an entry.

Implement a guided workflow:

**Choose Category → Choose Record Type → Complete Form → Review → Save**

Example categories:

### Government and Politics
- Legislative bill
- Parliamentary vote
- Executive order
- Government appointment
- Political party event
- Political investigation or conspiracy
- Cabinet decision
- Constitutional matter

### Military
- Military order
- Battle or after-action report
- Casualty report
- Equipment loss
- Unit formation
- Deployment
- Personnel appointment
- Military procurement
- Military reorganization

### Diplomacy
- Treaty
- Diplomatic incident
- Negotiation
- Foreign relations event
- International agreement

### Economy and Treasury
- Financial transaction
- Budget record
- Expenditure
- Government program
- Economic indicator
- Infrastructure project
- Debt or financing record

### Historical Archive
- Historical event
- Person
- Institution
- Organization
- Geographic location
- Source document

These categories should be driven by a typed record registry, not an enormous hardcoded component full of unrelated conditional logic.

## Guided forms

Each record type must have a purpose-built form with appropriate fields.

Every record should support, where relevant:

- Name or title.
- Historical effective date or date range.
- Description and notes.
- Existing related entities.
- Creating a missing related entity without abandoning the form.
- Source references and citations.
- Approval or canon status.
- Uncertainty and unknown values.
- Draft saving.
- Validation before final submission.

Do not force irrelevant fields onto every record.

Offer both a full guided form and a compact quick-entry option where appropriate.

## Review before saving

Show a human-readable review screen.

It should explain:

1. What record will be created.
2. Which related records it will link to.
3. Whether existing records will change.
4. What information remains unknown.
5. Whether the change requires canon approval.

Do not expose raw JSON in this review screen.

Use transactions and existing application services to prevent incomplete or duplicated writes.

---

# PHASE 3: REPAIR DISCONNECTED WORKFLOWS

Every record-creation workflow must be fully usable from its natural location.

For example:

- Create a new battle and its casualty report in the same workflow.
- Create a bill and link its sponsor without leaving the bill form.
- Appoint a new minister, selecting or creating the relevant person.
- Record a military deployment and associate it with an existing formation.
- Create a treaty and link the participating nations.
- Create historical events and associate multiple people, places, and organizations.

Use shared domain services rather than duplicating business logic across forms.

Do not introduce a generic record creator that ignores important differences between record types.

When necessary, use a guided multi-step workflow or inline entity-creation modal.

All changes must preserve historical accuracy, approval state, and source provenance.

---

# PHASE 4: VISUAL REDESIGN

RNAS currently feels cluttered, technical, and disconnected.

Give it a cohesive visual identity.

The target style is:

**A professional European national administration interface with the information density and clarity of a polished grand-strategy game.**

Use restrained institutional styling:

- Deep navy.
- Muted green.
- Warm ivory and limestone.
- Subtle brass accents.
- Elegant serif headings.
- Highly readable body typography.
- Consistent spacing and restrained borders.

Avoid:

- Endless grids of identical cards.
- Raw JSON as the main presentation.
- Technical database labels shown to the user.
- Redundant metadata.
- Giant empty panels.
- Excessive badges.
- Decorative graphics that serve no functional purpose.

Different departments should have distinct layouts appropriate to their tasks.

Maintain responsiveness and accessibility.

## Treasury redesign

Make the Treasury one of the most visually polished sections.

Introduce a cohesive financial dashboard with:

- Large, prominent headline economic figures.
- Revenue versus expenditure comparison.
- Projected fiscal deficit.
- Public debt.
- GDP and inflation.
- Spending allocations where verified data exists.
- Defense share of expenditure.
- Major infrastructure and government programs.
- Authorization versus committed versus paid expenditure.
- Tables for underlying transactions and programs.

Use appropriate interactive visualizations:

- Bar charts for comparisons.
- Stacked bars for allocations.
- Donut charts for distribution.
- Line charts only when actual chronological data exists.
- Progress indicators for project funding and implementation.

Use a clear visual hierarchy.

A user should be able to understand Reunion's fiscal position without opening eight separate assertion cards.

**CRITICAL: Do not modify any approved budget figures, defense allocations, GDP figures, or established economic records.**

This is a display and usability task, not an economic canon revision.

Charts must derive from existing records. Do not fabricate numbers, time series, or categories.

Distinguish budget authorization, commitments, actual spending, and projections.

Historical date filters must be respected.

## Parliament redesign

Create a proper parliamentary workspace.

Features should include:

- An attractive semicircular seating chart showing the actual parliamentary composition.
- Party-colored seats and an informative legend.
- Party totals, percentages and historical composition.
- Individual political party profiles.
- Legislation and bill tracking.
- Voting history.
- Legislative calendar or historical event list.
- Current parliamentary status.
- Searchable member and bill registries.

Preserve the historical distinction between the actual 400-seat Parliament and the constitutional 300-seat maximum.

The application should explain the constitutional discrepancy rather than normalize it away.

The October 3, 1992 parliamentary suspension must be presented appropriately in historical views.

Avoid using the Parliament page as another generic database list.

Use meaningful visualizations, but keep important tables easily accessible.

## Cabinet and Government redesign

Create a polished official government directory.

At the top, display the President and Vice President.

Below, display the ministries in a compact, organized roster.

Each ministry should show:

- Official ministry name.
- Current minister.
- Office title.
- Appointment date if known.
- Party affiliation if established.
- Vacancy where appropriate.

Provide clickable minister and ministry profiles.

Support historical officeholders and appointments.

Do not invent portraits, biographical information, or missing officials.

Never show internal labels such as `baseline_minister` as a public-facing office title.

The government interface must distinguish the monarchy, presidency, Cabinet, and ministries appropriately.

Ensure officeholder records are valid for the selected historical date.

## Military redesign

Present the military using appropriate operational and organizational views.

Create:

- Searchable order of battle.
- Expandable formation hierarchy.
- Unit detail screens.
- Equipment and manpower tables.
- Deployment register.
- Battle and casualty history.
- Military order register.

Do not build a combat simulator in this phase.

Focus on reliable historical and administrative records.

---

# PHASE 5: SEARCH, NAVIGATION, AND INFORMATION DENSITY

Add universal search capable of locating:

- People.
- Organizations.
- Government offices.
- Bills.
- Treaties.
- Military units.
- Battles.
- Places.
- Historical events.
- Economic programs.

Search results should link directly to their record detail pages.

Make navigation consistent.

Use three levels of information:

1. Overview: the information most useful at a glance.
2. Registry and detail: complete operational and administrative records.
3. Evidence: source texts, provenance, import details, and historical assertions.

Raw technical information belongs in the evidence layer, not in prominent overview cards.

All major sections should have an obvious primary action for creating records.

---

# PHASE 6: RELIABLE IMPORT AND EXPORT

Establish portable export and import capabilities.

Support:

- Full database backup and restoration.
- Structured JSON export of supported records.
- Bulk exports by subsystem.
- Human-readable historical reports.
- Imported record validation.
- Duplicate detection.
- Explicit change previews.
- Historical source and provenance preservation.

Design the import/export format so a future separate military campaign simulator can exchange records with RNAS.

Do not build that simulator or live integration yet.

An external campaign should eventually be able to export battles, movements, losses, equipment changes, and after-action reports for review before canon approval.

Do not automatically import hypothetical results as established history.

Keep versioned, documented data formats.

---

# DEVELOPMENT RULES

1. Preserve approved Reunion canon and all existing records.
2. Do not modify the established national budget.
3. Preserve and appropriately resolve the known Altis 1993 dating issue, which resulted from a mistyped year. Do not indiscriminately rewrite other 1993 records.
4. Reuse existing database and domain services when appropriate.
5. Do not create a parallel database architecture.
6. Do not rewrite working subsystems without justification.
7. Avoid large, monolithic components.
8. Keep record schemas and form definitions modular.
9. Verify database persistence and backup/restore behavior.
10. Add tests for record creation, approval, data relationships, historical dates, and import/export.
11. Use existing architectural documentation and update implementation status and session handoff notes.

Work in small, testable increments.

Do not attempt to deliver every phase in a single uncontrolled change.

Prioritize the unified record-creation interface, followed by repairs to disconnected workflows, then Treasury, Parliament, Cabinet, and search.

Preserve working features while improving them.

The goal is not a larger RNAS.

**The goal is a cleaner, more complete, easier-to-use RNAS that can serve as Reunion's authoritative national database for years of continued worldbuilding.**
