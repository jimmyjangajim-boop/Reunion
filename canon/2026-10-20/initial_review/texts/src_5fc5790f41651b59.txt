[4] Front Page
[5] PRESIDENT DONAHUE SIGNS FOUR KEY POSTWAR LAWS
 New Framework for Veterans, Reconstruction, Emergencies, and April 6th Accountability
[6] Reunion City – President Dax Donahue yesterday signed four major pieces of legislation into law, in what observers are calling the first coherent phase of the postwar settlement.
[7] The bills, passed by the Assembly between June 25th and July 1st, are:
[8] Evinos & Marine Pocket Veterans Relief Act
[9] Establishes disability pensions and medical support for wounded veterans of Evinos and the Marine Pocket, as well as stipends for widows and orphans.
[10] Creates a unified National Veterans Registry to standardize claims and prevent fraud.
[11] Post-War Reconstruction & Stabilization Fund Act
[12] Creates a 10-year Reconstruction Fund, initially capitalized at 250 million RMC.
[13] Prioritizes critical infrastructure on all three main islands, with early focus on ports, roads, and power in Reunion City and Torrental.
[14] Introduces a “soft” deficit ceiling, signaling intent to keep public finances under control.
[15] Emergency Powers & Martial Law Sunset Act
[16] Formally ends martial law in Reunion City and other affected areas on a fixed timetable unless renewed by a supermajority of the Assembly.
[17] Tightens rules around states of emergency, requiring shorter initial durations and more frequent parliamentary review.
[18] Limits extended pre-charge detention and mandates reporting on emergency operations.
[19] Families of April 6th Victims Compensation & Policing Review Act
[20] Provides compensation schemes for families of those killed on April 6th—police, soldiers, security staff, and civilians.
[21] Establishes an independent commission to review public-order policing tactics and recommend reforms to use-of-force protocols.
[22] In a brief signing statement, President Donahue described the package as “the beginning of a long process of repair—of our infrastructure, of our institutions, and of our conscience.”
[24] Second Lead
[25] PALACE, JUSTICE MINISTRY FLOAT ‘CONSTITUTIONAL HOUSEKEEPING’
[26] Reunion City – Legal and political circles are buzzing after it emerged that the Ministry of Justice, in coordination with palace legal advisers, has requested a cabinet discussion next week on what is being described as “technical modernization of constitutional language.”
[27] Sources in the Assembly say the discussion will focus on:
[28] Clarifying the Crown’s consultative role in matters of war, peace, constitutional amendments, and key appointments,
[29] Harmonizing emergency provisions with current practice,
[30] Codifying the balance of powers between the Presidency, the Assembly, and the monarchy.
[31] Officials emphasize there is “no question” of abolishing the presidency or overturning the republic. Supporters argue that outdated 1946 wording needs to be brought in line with the evolved role of the Crown as a “guardian of continuity.”
[32] Privately, some opposition figures worry that vague language about “moral guardianship” could, over time, give the palace more leverage over legislation and security policy than is currently acknowledged.
[33] A full cabinet session on the topic is expected in early July.
[35] Politics & Parties
[36] ROYALIST PARTY SIGNALS IDEOLOGICAL SHIFT AHEAD OF EXTRAORDINARY CONGRESS
[37] Reunion City – The Royalist Conservative Alliance (RCA) executive committee has approved draft changes to its program ahead of an extraordinary party congress planned for later this year.
[38] The new platform language:
[39] Places stronger emphasis on “national identity anchored in the Crown,”
[40] Calls for a harder line against terrorism, separatism, and foreign interference,
[41] Elevates traditional values, family, and faith as pillars of the state,
[42] Frames the monarchy as “the living symbol of Reunion’s unity and historical continuity.”
[43] Several senior RCA figures insist the party remains “firmly democratic,” but admit that the tone is shifting to capture disaffected nationalist and security-focused voters who have drifted toward the NSF in recent years.
[44] Observers in Parliament note that Royalist MPs have become more vocal in debates on internal security and culture, presenting themselves as the “serious patriotic alternative” between leftist populism and militarized hardliners.
[46] Economy & Society
[47] RECONSTRUCTION FUND RAISES HOPES AND QUESTIONS
[48] Reunion City – Business associations and trade unions cautiously welcomed the Post-War Reconstruction & Stabilization Fund, but questions remain about priorities and oversight.
[49] Finance Ministry estimates suggest the Fund, together with veterans’ programs, could reach around 1% of GDP per year in new spending during the first phase.
[50] Economists close to the government argue that:
[51] If directed toward high-impact projects (ports, rail, power),
[52] And paired with tighter control of wasteful legacy programs,
[53] the spending could boost long-term growth and employment.
[54] Skeptics warn that without strong anti-corruption safeguards, the Fund risks becoming “another sink for favors and political construction contracts.”
[55] A separate Public Procurement & Anti-Corruption Reform Act, strengthening transparency and oversight of government contracts, is reported to be advancing through committee and could reach the Assembly floor later in July.
[57] Security & Internal Affairs
[58] INTELLIGENCE OVERSIGHT BILL GAINS MOMENTUM
[59] Reunion City – The Interim Intelligence Oversight & Reporting Act, which would create a temporary parliamentary subcommittee to oversee RIA and GRI activities and demand a full inventory of their programs, has passed key committee stages and is expected to see a plenary vote in the coming weeks.
[60] The bill is widely seen as a precursor to a deeper reorganization of the intelligence community, including the proposed creation of a more clearly civilian Reunion National Intelligence Agency (RNIA).
[61] Government spokespeople present the move as “normalization after war,” while critics warn against weakening internal security as extremist cells and foreign influence operations continue to probe the country’s defenses.
[63] World & Foreign Desk
[64] MOSCOW STRAINS TO PROJECT POWER AFTER MEDITERRANEAN SETBACK
[65] Moscow – Soviet leadership is struggling to manage the fallout from recent naval losses in the Mediterranean, including the near-destruction of its operational submarine force in the theater.
[66] Official statements from Premier Somov Antonovich emphasize “strategic patience” and highlight economic recovery plans, but party insiders and foreign diplomats report:
[67] Intensifying internal debates between economic reformers and hardline security figures,
[68] Renewed emphasis on covert and proxy operations abroad, particularly in southern Europe and friendly regimes.
[69] While large-scale Soviet military action against Reunion is widely considered unlikely in the short term, intelligence sources in several Western capitals note increased activity by Soviet advisers and covert operatives in People’s Greece, the Balkans, and sympathetic movements across the Mediterranean.
[71] UNITED STATES CONSOLIDATES GULF POSITION, WATCHES MOSCOW AND ATHENS
[72] Washington – The United States continues to solidify its military foothold in the Persian Gulf after the defeat of Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait.
[73] Key developments:
[74] No-fly zones over Iraq remain in effect.
[75] US and allied naval forces maintain persistent patrols to secure shipping lanes.
[76] American analysts track Soviet moves closely, wary of any renewed Moscow–Baghdad or Moscow–Damascus arms channels.
[77] In southern Europe, US policy circles watch the Reunion–Soviet confrontation with mixed feelings: relief that Reunion has held its ground at sea, but concern that repeated crises could drag NATO into a larger confrontation.
[78] Unofficially, Washington views Reunion as a useful, if unpredictable, partner—valuable in checking Soviet influence, but risky if domestic instability or overreach triggers a wider escalation.
[80] BALKANS REMAIN UNSTABLE AS CROATIA CONSOLIDATES, BOSNIA FRACTURES
[81] Zagreb / Sarajevo – Croatia, having declared independence and fought multiple engagements against the collapsing Yugoslav federal structures, appears to be consolidating control over much of its claimed territory after a series of ceasefires.
[82] However:
[83] Bosnia is fracturing among rival factions with ethnic and ideological dimensions;
[84] Various “volunteer” formations, some with foreign backing, continue to move across porous borders.
[85] Regional analysts warn that Dalmatia under Reunion administration remains an exposed flank, with militant groups and criminal syndicates reportedly rearming and re-organizing in the shadows.
[87] PEOPLE’S GREECE: CIVIL CONFLICT GRINDS ON
[88] Athens / Thessaly – The communist government in People’s Greece continues to fight a draining conflict against insurgent formations such as the Hellenic Revival Movement.
[89] Recent weeks saw:
[90] Night raids, ambushes, and retaliatory operations in northern regions,
[91] Conflicting claims by state media and underground broadcasters over casualties and territory,
[92] Signs of Soviet advisers operating more openly in some sectors, according to Western intelligence assessments.
[93] No comprehensive settlement appears in sight; the conflict remains a slow bleed on both the Greek regime and its Soviet backers.
[95] Short Domestic Notes
[96] Rail Modernization Debate: The Transport Ministry is reportedly preparing a major proposal designating a Lucania–Steinberg–Reunion City–Torrental corridor as a strategic rail priority, with discussions of eventual electrification and faster passenger service. Branch lines would be upgraded to diesel in phases.
[97] Royal Ceremonies: The Palace has announced a sequence of remembrance events for Evinos and the Marine Pocket, as well as the April 6th victims. Several decorated officers and veterans’ representatives are expected to attend in prominent roles.
[98] Fuel Prices: A Temporary Fuel & Energy Price Stabilization bill is being discussed in committee amid growing public concern about rising household costs. Energy and Finance officials say they are “studying options” to cushion price shocks without permanently distorting markets.
