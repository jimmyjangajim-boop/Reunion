[1] 1. National Housing Restitution & Urban Renewal Act (NHRURA)
[2] Date: Passed July 12
[3] What it does:
[4] Creates a Housing Restitution Fund to:
[5] Compensate families whose homes were destroyed or severely damaged during the war / April 6th unrest.
[6] Co-finance rebuilding in burned-out urban districts of Reunion City, Steinberg, and some Dalmatian towns.
[7] Allows:
[8] Public–private “urban renewal zones” where the state funds infrastructure and private developers build housing/shops under set quotas for affordable units.
[9] Who backed it:
[10] RRP – sees it as socially stabilizing and pro-order.
[11] LUP – likes the compensation and affordable housing quotas.
[12] RCA (moderate wing) – supports to calm tensions and show the Crown’s concern.
[13] Who grumbled but voted yes:
[14] NSF – worried about “rewarding” neighborhoods they associate with unrest, but ultimately supports to avoid looking openly anti-civilian.
[16] 2. National Values & Civic Education Implementation Decree Act (NVCEIDA)
[17] Date: Passed July 13
[18] What it does:
[19] Puts meat on the vague earlier “civic education” law:
[20] Mandates a new unified history/civics curriculum for secondary schools.
[21] Requires modules on:
[22] The civil war,
[23] Evinos & Marine Pocket,
[24] “The historical continuity of the Crown and the Republic.”
[25] Creates a Civic Education Council:
[26] Members appointed by Education, with “consultative representation” from:
[27] Palace cultural office,
[28] Veterans’ associations,
[29] Academic institutions.
[30] Who backed it:
[31] RCA / Royalists – they essentially wrote half of it through palace-adjacent advisors.
[32] NSF – supports a strong patriotic curriculum.
[33] Some RRP moderates – support the “shared civic identity” line.
[34] Who opposed / abstained:
[35] Most of LUP and some RRP liberals, warning it’s a vehicle for biased narrative and Crown glorification.
[37] 3. Veterans Security Employment & Firearms Licensing Act (VSEFLA)
[38] Date: Passed July 14
[39] What it does:
[40] Streamlines firearms licensing and background checks for:
[41] Honorably discharged veterans of Evinos and Marine Pocket.
[42] Incentivizes private security companies to:
[43] Hire veterans by offering payroll tax reductions.
[44] Allows veterans with clean records to:
[45] Fast-track into police, port security, and rail security jobs.
[46] Who backed it:
[47] NSF – loves anything that arms “trusted men” and expands security.
[48] RCA – frames it as honoring veterans and bolstering order.
[49] RRP – split, but leadership supports on the grounds of employment and reducing vagrancy among vets.
[50] Who opposed:
[51] LUP and civil liberties MPs – afraid of creeping paramilitarization and politicized private security outfits.
[53] 4. Coastal Border Security & Customs Enhancement Act (CBSEA)
[54] Date: Passed July 16
[55] What it does:
[56] Adds funding for:
[57] Extra customs officers,
[58] Small patrol craft,
[59] Better scanners at major ports (Reunion City, Torrental, Lucania, Dalmatian ports).
[60] Gives Interior and Maritime ministries:
[61] Expanded legal powers to interdict suspicious cargo,
[62] Longer detention windows for contraband investigations.
[63] Who backed it:
[64] NSF, RRP, RCA – everyone sells it as “fighting smuggling and arms into Dalmatia / REF cells.”
[65] Maritime & Fisheries ministry lobbied hard.
[66] Who opposed:
[67] Small number of LUP and libertarian-ish deputies worried about harassment of small traders and fishermen.
[69] 5. Domestic Investment Incentives (Strategic Sectors) Act (DIISSA)
[70] Date: Passed July 19
[71] What it does:
[72] Offers targeted tax credits and accelerated depreciation for investments in:
[73] Rail rolling stock and infrastructure,
[74] Energy equipment (including nuclear supply chain),
[75] Heavy machinery and industrial automation.
[76] Only applies to:
[77] Companies that commit to local hiring and some minimum capital outlay.
[78] Who backed it:
[79] RRP – industrial policy win.
[80] RCA business wing – loves tax incentives.
[81] Curro quietly supports because it’s focused rather than scattershot.
[82] Who opposed:
[83] Harder LUP members – see it as a partial rollback of tax-break reforms.
[84] A few fiscal hawks worried it undermines the clean-up of tax expenditures.
[86] 1. Defense & Intelligence Procurement Transparency Amendment (DIPTA)
[87] Status: Stuck in committee as of July 20
[88] What it would do:
[89] Amends PPACRA to explicitly cover:
[90] All defense and intelligence procurement, with only narrow security exceptions.
[91] Requires:
[92] A confidential but audited registry of all secret/deniable contracts, accessible to:
[93] A small, cross-party Parliamentary Security Committee,
[94] The Auditor-General’s office.
[95] Mandates:
[96] Annual classified reports on:
[97] Special forces, covert intel projects, and their associated spending lines.
[98] Who backs it:
[99] RRP reformists,
[100] LUP,
[101] Halvik’s Justice Ministry,
[102] Curro (quietly) – because it closes a massive budget black hole.
[103] Who is killing it:
[104] NSF,
[105] Old RIA networks,
[106] Some RCA security hawks,
[107] Anyone with fingers in off-book defense business.
[108] Behind the scenes:
[109] Hartmann’s people are working overtime to bury this as “national security suicide.”
[111] 2. Critical Infrastructure Sovereign Safeguard Act (CISSA)
[112] Status: Partially gutted, key clauses stalled as of July 20
[113] What it would do (in its original form):
[114] Declares key infrastructure assets “strategic and inalienable,” including:
[115] Main ports,
[116] SHSC rail corridor,
[117] Power grid and major plants (including Lucania).
[118] Requires:
[119] Any sale/privatization/long lease to foreign or domestic conglomerates to be:
[120] Approved by a 2/3 majority in Parliament,
[121] Reviewed by RNIA for security risks,
[122] Subject to public summary disclosure.
[123] Establishes:
[124] A Sovereign Infrastructure Fund to hold state stakes and prevent quiet carve-ups.
[125] Who backs it:
[126] RRP,
[127] Royalists (ironically, they like the “national patrimony” angle),
[128] Elements of LUP who fear foreign sell-offs.
[129] Who attacks it:
[130] Big business/oligarch circles wanting:
[131] Port concessions,
[132] Future rail privatization,
[133] Energy monopoles.
[134] Some NSF and “strong state” types:
[135] Worried about tying the executive’s hands in a contingency,
[136] And quietly fond of the ability to hand goodies to loyal cronies or foreign friends when convenient.
[137] Status by July 20:
[138] Core “2/3 approval + RNIA review” requirements:
[139] Stalled and under “redrafting” in an economic subcommittee.
[140] A watered-down version that just says “strategic” without strong locks:
[141] Might pass later, but the real teeth are on ice.
