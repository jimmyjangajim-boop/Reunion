[1] Here’s how everyone else reacts in early October 1992 when Strevo drops the “Horizon Dynamics” consolidation bomb and the Crown piles in.
[2] 1) Parliament’s immediate posture
[3] RRP (centrist institutionalists)
[4] Not opposed, but they refuse to rubber-stamp a “royal + Curro megacorp” during a credibility crisis.
[5] They push for hard guardrails: procurement transparency, audited capitalization, conflict-of-interest walls, and export controls consistent with state policy.
[6] RCA (royalists)
[7] All-in, loudly. They frame it as “national revival” and “sovereignty through industry.”
[8] They want Royla and Crown-linked suppliers in the ecosystem (steel, ports, security).
[9] NSF (security-nationalists)
[10] All-in, but with strings: security vetting, closed committees, and “strategic secrecy” exemptions.
[11] They push for Horizon to become the backbone of a permanent mobilization economy.
[12] LUP (labor/left)
[13] Split:
[14] pro-jobs wing supports consolidation if it means domestic employment, worker protections, and civilian tech spillovers.
[15] civil-liberties wing treats it as a “royal-industrial coup by balance sheet” and demands oversight and limits.
[16] Dalmatian lists/minors
[17] Mostly transactional: they want guarantees that Horizon’s truck/logistics production benefits Dalmatia supply chains, not just the core islands.
[18] Net result: Parliament moves fast, but only if it can brand this as “national champion + strict oversight”, not a carve-up.
[20] 2) What Finance/Justice/Energy demand before they’ll let it proceed
[21] Even with Strevo offering transparency, the system will force structure, because the political cost of “ethics panic” is real.
[22] The required “clean-room” conditions
[23] Ownership clarity: Strevo’s 70% net worth injection can’t sit in a murky bucket. The clean solution the Finance Committee demands is:
[24] Strevo funds Horizon via non-voting preferred equity or convertible debt (publicly disclosed terms),
[25] while Curro (70%) and the King (30%) hold the voting common shares only if Curro places his stake in a blind trust / managed holding while he’s Finance Minister.
[26] Procurement firewall: Horizon cannot bid on state contracts unless:
[27] tenders are run under PPACRA rules,
[28] and an independent procurement panel signs off.
[29] Export control compliance: Parliament inserts language that all sensitive exports require Cabinet approval and must follow “Reunion strategic interest” rules.
[30] Security oversight: RNIA/Interior require:
[31] vetting of all foreign technical staff,
[32] and strict control of remote diagnostics and comms systems.
[33] Translation: Parliament won’t stop it, but it will chain it to oversight so it doesn’t become the country’s next corruption engine.
[35] 3) The bill that actually passes (early October)
[36] BILL No. 207/1992 — Horizon Dynamics National Consolidation & Mobilization Act (HDNCMA)
[37] Passed: 9 October 1992
 Sponsors: RCA + NSF + RRP modernization bloc (with LUP concessions)
 Key provisions:
[38] Authorizes mergers/buyouts of designated defense-industrial firms into Horizon Dynamics.
[39] Creates a Strategic Industrial Board (Finance + Energy + Defense + Justice + one parliamentary oversight seat) with veto power over:
[40] major acquisitions,
[41] related-party contracts,
[42] export destinations,
[43] and any “dual-use electronics” procurement.
[44] Requires public disclosure of capitalization structure, beneficial ownership, and annual audited accounts.
[45] Mandates local content and workforce training pipelines (HTESA tie-in).
[46] Vote sketch (typical outcome):
[47] RRP: strong majority for (with a chunk abstaining over conflicts)
[48] RCA/NSF: overwhelmingly for
[49] LUP: split (jobs wing votes yes; civil-liberties wing votes no/abstain)
[50] Dalmatian lists: mostly yes if logistics guarantees included
[52] 4) Who gets bought into Horizon (what survives)
[53] The Ministry of Energy & Industry’s consolidation list targets the “still-alive” shells:
[54] Lucania Motor Works (vehicle assembly + heavy diesel capability)
[55] Steinberg Ordnance & Foundry (metalwork, castings, barrels, industrial machining)
[56] Reunion Avionics & Instruments Group (electronics, radios, basic radar components)
[57] Torrental Heavy Logistics Works (trailers, cranes, port equipment)
[58] Reunion Systems Integration Bureau (small R&D office that becomes Horizon’s “brain”)
[59] Parliament insists a few civilian-leaning subsidiaries stay separately audited to prevent military slush funds from swallowing everything.
[61] 5) The four “big projects” — how the state reacts
[62] Project 1: Heavy logistics trucks (MAZ-537-like, plus smaller variant)
[63] Reception: best received of the four. Everyone understands trucks = logistics = survival.
 Defense and Infrastructure committees immediately earmark it as a priority because:
[64] it supports Dalmatia supply resilience,
[65] it supports rail/port reconstruction,
[66] it can export regionally later.
[67] Problem ministries flag: steel supply, engine supply chain, and QA discipline.
[68] Project 2: Radar competitive with a NATO market
[69] Reception: politically popular, technically hard.
 The military wants it; RRP wants “exportable industry”; but engineers warn:
[70] “competitive NATO radar” is not a 12–18 month win; it’s multi-year R&D + supply chain maturity.
[71] So Parliament frames it as:
[72] Phase 1: reliable coastal/ground surveillance radars for Reunion first
[73] Phase 2: export-grade systems later if credible
[74] Project 3: Broad electronics modernization capability
[75] Reception: universally supported if it stays dual-use.
 NDIDMA and HTESA give it cover: “modern chips, secure comms, industrial controls.”
 Justice Ministry demands strict compliance language so this doesn’t become an illegal procurement circus.
[76] Project 4: “Harpyiae” air-superiority fighter using MiG wreckage + a modern French jet purchase
[77] Reception: the most controversial.
[78] Defense hawks love the dream, but the professional military/technocrats call it what it is: expensive, slow, and politically risky.
[79] France will sell aircraft, but they’ll insist on end-use controls and will not bless a scheme that looks like cloning or tech diversion.
[80] RNIA and Justice are alarmed by anything resembling backdoor acquisition of controlled US components.
[81] So Parliament’s likely compromise:
[82] Approve purchase of a modern French air-superiority platform for immediate capability (e.g., Mirage 2000C/B era) under strict terms, and
[83] authorize a long-range domestic R&D program focused on avionics, materials, and flight testing—without pretending a clean-sheet air-superiority fighter appears quickly.
