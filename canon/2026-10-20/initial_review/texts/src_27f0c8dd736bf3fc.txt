[1] REUNION OIL
[3] 1) What Parliament “goes for” (the law that passes)
[4] BILL No. 201/1992 — Offshore Petroleum Sovereignty & Strategic Development Act (OPSSDA)
[5] Passed: 6 October 1992
 Sponsors: RRP (institutionalists) + RCA (industrial royalists) + NSF (security caucus) with negotiated LUP add-ons
 Committees: Energy & Industry; Finance; National Security; Public Procurement (oversight insertions)
[6] Core deal inside the bill
[7] 80% Nationalized / 20% Privatized by production entitlement (not land ownership).
[8] The state keeps a permanent 80% participating interest through a new NOC holding vehicle.
[9] Private firms compete for slices of the 20% production share via tenders.
[10] Export control: private production can only be sold to approved markets/buyers set by Cabinet (and can be restricted in crisis).
[11] Domestic supply obligation: a fixed minimum share must be offered to Reunion first at indexed prices (to prevent domestic energy blackmail).
[12] Signature bonus + FX terms: every winning consortium pays an upfront signature bonus (in USD/ECU or equivalent hard currency) into a ring-fenced Stabilization Account.
[13] Security vetting: all foreign technical staff on critical assets get Interior/RNIA vetting.
[14] Fast-track permitting: timelines are compressed, but “minimum safety” language stays (LUP forced that in).
[15] Greece clause (quiet but crucial): development in the shared basin is contingent on a unitization / joint development understanding for the portion that lies between Reunion and Greece / in Greek waters.
[16] Committee fingerprints
[17] RRP: forced competitive tender rules + public registry of contract awards.
[18] RCA: carved out a “National Champion Preference” scoring category (this is how Royla doesn’t get boxed out).
[19] NSF: security veto power on foreign staff and remote diagnostics systems.
[20] LUP: forced a domestic supply obligation + worker training requirements + a modest environmental floor.
[21] Vote (400 seats)
[22] RRP: 106 For / 8 Against / 4 Abstain
[23] RCA: 74 For / 8 Against / 0 Abstain
[24] LUP: 61 For / 28 Against / 6 Abstain
[25] NSF: 56 For / 4 Against / 2 Abstain
[26] Dalmatian lists/minors: 23 For / 14 Against / 2 Abstain
 Total: 320 For / 62 Against / 14 Abstain (4 absent)
[27] Why it passes: everyone wants the story that “Reunion has a solvent future,” even if they’re terrified of who gets rich.

[93] 1.2M × 365 = 438,000,000 barrels/year (438M bbl/yr)
[94] If you could magically “switch” the stream from heavy to light (i.e., it sells like a light sweet crude instead of taking a heavy discount), the revenue jump is basically:
 (heavy discount removed) × 438M barrels, then apply your state take.
[95] Key assumption (the only thing that really matters)
[96] Heavy crude typically sells at a discount to benchmark light crude. A reasonable planning range is:
[97] Heavy discount vs light: $8–$15 per barrel (sometimes more, but this is a solid “planning band”)
[99] 1) Government revenue at 1.2M bpd if sold as “light”
[100] Earlier, for heavy at 1.2M bpd, I gave a government take of ~$4.27B/yr (based on a mid state-take and heavy netback).
[101] If the oil sells as light, your government take increases by roughly:
[102] Extra gov $/bbl = discount removed × state take
[103] Use 55–65% as a normal state take band
[104] Extra government revenue from switching heavy → light
[105] Low case: $8 × 55% = $4.40/bbl → 438M × 4.40 = $1.93B/yr
[106] High case: $15 × 65% = $9.75/bbl → 438M × 9.75 = $4.27B/yr
[107] New government revenue (light equivalent)
[108] Add that to the heavy-case $4.27B/yr:
[109] Low case: $4.27B + $1.93B = $6.20B/yr
[110] High case: $4.27B + $4.27B = $8.54B/yr
[111] Convert to RMC at $1 = 5.8 RMC:
[112] $6.20B → 35.96B RMC/yr
[113] $8.54B → 49.53B RMC/yr
[114] So: light-equivalent at 1.2M bpd = roughly $6.2B–$8.5B per year to the state, or ~36B–50B RMC/yr.
