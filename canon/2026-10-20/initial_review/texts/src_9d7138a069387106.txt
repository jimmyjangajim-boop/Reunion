 Good evening. This is RBD. [7] Segment 1 — Dalmatian Peace Deal Holds… For Now
[8] [ROLL VIDEO: map graphic of Dalmatian coast; Zadar highlighted down to just north of Šibenik]
[9] ANCHOR:
 One month after the ceasefire collapsed into renewed combat, Reunion and Croatia signed a Croatian-drafted peace instrument on the date the ceasefire ended—locking in a new line of control and a 12-year “non-war” clause.
[10] Under the deal: Croatia assumes administration of Zadar southward to a line just north of Šibenik, and Reunion forces are permitted to withdraw under safe-passage provisions, including explicit language requiring Croatian forces not to be fired upon during withdrawal.
[11] But analysts inside the Assembly are warning the text is deliberately brittle—its breach clauses broad enough that “any little thing can break it,” as one senior committee member put it today.
[12] [CUT TO: Parliament hallway scrum]
[13] RBD PARLIAMENT CORRESPONDENT:
 The governing blocs are publicly calling this “a pause to rebuild,” but privately, MPs describe it as a treaty written with tripwires. The RRP’s moderates are pushing for strict compliance protocols and incident reporting to avoid accidental breach. The NSF calls it “temporary paper,” while the RCA claims it prevents outright collapse of the front and buys time.
[15] Segment 2 — Economy: A Confidence Crisis, Not Just a Budget Crisis
[16] [ROLL GRAPHIC: GDP in RMC and USD equivalent; “2.9T RMC → ~2.4T RMC”]
[17] ANCHOR:
 Reunion’s economy has taken a sharp hit since the start of Donahue’s term. Treasury estimates now place GDP around 2.4 trillion RMC, down from 2.9 trillion RMC—a decline that roughly corresponds to a fall from ~$500 billion to ~$414 billion at the current conversion rate of 5.8 RMC to $1.
[18] Finance officials emphasize the drop is being driven by a cascade:
[19] war disruption and shipping insurance risk,
[20] business closures and tightening credit,
[21] softening tax collections as trade slows and firms delay payments,
[22] and confidence damage—with households hoarding and markets demanding higher risk premiums.
[23] [ROLL B-ROLL: shuttered storefronts, people lining up at banks, port cranes idle]
[24] RBD ECONOMICS DESK:
 Even as the government raises ministry budgets and accelerates spending commitments—ports, rail, veterans support, food stabilization—revenue reliability is worsening. Analysts stress: this isn’t only about “how much is spent,” but whether the state can keep ports functioning, keep fuel and parts moving, and prevent further shocks to credibility.
[26] Segment 3 — Offshore Oil: Parliament Moves Fast on “80/20”
[27] [ROLL VIDEO: offshore survey ship, sonar screens, then a map of the basin]
[28] ANCHOR:
 The biggest story driving market speculation this week: the newly confirmed offshore light-oil deposits. Parliament has moved quickly to codify the political line: 80% remains nationalized, 20% is privatized by production entitlement, and all private operators must comply with Reunion’s strategic export policy—selling only to approved markets.
[29] The legislation includes:
[30] a domestic supply obligation,
[31] hard-currency signature bonuses paid into a ring-fenced stabilization account,
[32] and security vetting of foreign technical staff.
[33] RBD ENERGY CORRESPONDENT:
 The mood in the Assembly is “drill, but with chains on it.” Everyone wants the solvency narrative, but no one wants to be seen handing sovereignty to foreign majors. Committees have also flagged the diplomatic complication: a significant portion of the deposits sits in waters between Reunion and Greece and within Greek waters—meaning long-term development will require unitization or joint development arrangements.
[35] Segment 4 — Who’s Bidding: Shell, Exxon, France—And the Domestic Bloc
[36] [ROLL GRAPHIC: “Privatized 20% divided into tranches; winners pending/announced in principle”]
[37] ANCHOR:
 Lobbying has surged. Major international bidders have appeared in Reunion City in force—Shell and Exxon leading the charge, with French bidders also pressing offers packaged with training and downstream support. Parliamentary sources say the goal is to split awards to avoid dependence on any single foreign operator.
[38] Domestic plays
[39] Younger Industries has mounted the largest private domestic bid, framing itself as “the national industrial partner.”
[40] Royla, the new oil arm of the Crown’s expanding holdings network, has secured a stake roughly equivalent to 2% of Reunion’s total oil entitlement—a smaller slice than some expected, but enough to secure board influence and long-term cashflow.
[41] Other domestic industrial groups are positioning for service contracts: rigs, supply vessels, depot operations, and refining logistics.
[42] RBD BUSINESS DESK:
 The risk now is political: oil can stabilize the currency in expectation, but expectations collapse if Parliament fractures over corruption fears—or if export control and revenue ring-fencing are seen as unenforced.
[44] Segment 5 — Horizon Dynamics: One Company to Build the Next Era of War Industry
[45] [ROLL VIDEO: factory floors, truck chassis, radar arrays, machinists in Steinberg and Lucania]
[46] ANCHOR:
 In another major move, Parliament has authorized a consolidation of surviving defense-industrial firms into a single national champion: Horizon Dynamics.
[47] According to committee briefings, Horizon has already begun absorbing:
[48] Lucania Motor Works (heavy vehicle assembly),
[49] Steinberg Ordnance & Foundry (machining and castings),
[50] Reunion Avionics & Instruments Group (electronics and sensors),
[51] Torrental Heavy Logistics Works (trailers, cranes, port equipment),
[52] and a small systems integration bureau now rebranded as Horizon’s R&D spine.
[53] The stated priorities, per Parliamentary committee summaries:
[54] Heavy logistics trucks emphasizing power and reliability over comfort
[55] Radar investment focused first on domestic coastal and ground surveillance
[56] Modern electronics capacity to reduce dependence on imports
[57] Early-stage air power R&D framed as a “long program,” not an immediate replacement fleet
[58] RBD DEFENSE-INDUSTRY CORRESPONDENT:
 There is broad support for the truck program because it directly feeds logistics in both peacetime and crisis. Radar and electronics are seen as slow-burn sovereignty projects. The aviation ambition is the most controversial—committees are insisting on compliance controls and realistic timelines to avoid a prestige sink.
[60] Segment 6 — Royla and the Crown’s New Holdings Structure
[61] [ROLL GRAPHIC: “Royla Holdings” / “Royla Oil” / minority stakes in logistics and security services]
[62] ANCHOR:
 And now to Royla Holdings, the Crown-aligned investment structure that now owns Royla Oil and a growing web of strategic minority positions—ports logistics, supply services, and energy-adjacent contracts. Royalist MPs call it “national capital mobilization.” Critics call it “a parallel state economy.”
[63] In Parliament, the debate is not whether Royla exists—its existence is now fact. The fight is over:
[64] procurement firewalls,
[65] beneficial ownership disclosures,
[66] and whether the new national champions serve the republic—or slowly bend it.
[68] Segment 7 — The National Mood
[69] [ROLL B-ROLL: a market, a recruiting line, a train yard, a quiet church, a closed factory gate]
[70] ANCHOR:
 Public sentiment remains strained: war fatigue, fear of renewed fighting, and deep anxiety about jobs and prices. But oil has changed something subtle: for the first time in weeks, even hardened opposition MPs have stopped saying “we’re finished” and started saying “we might have a way out.”
[71] The question now is whether Parliament can keep unity long enough to turn that prospect into reality.
[73] Bosnia Seeks Foreign Backing; UN Inquiry into Ploče Proposed
[74] ANCHOR:
 RBD has learned that Bosnia’s new leadership has approached both the United States and Saudi Arabia in separate channels requesting peacekeepers and emergency support, as tensions continue to rise across the region.
[75] A senior diplomatic source confirms that the United States has offered to push for a United Nations investigation into the Ploče incident, signaling growing international attention on the confrontation surrounding the port city and the competing accounts of what occurred there.
[76] At the same time, Bosnia has announced that Iraq, Syria, and Libya have jointly delivered what Bosnia describes as two billion dollars’ worth of weapons and military equipment. Bosnian officials claim the shipments are intended to strengthen Bosnia’s ability to fight Reunion, framing the conflict as a defensive struggle and using religious language—calling it a “jihad” and accusing Reunion of aggression.
[77] Reunion officials have not issued a full formal response to the claims of foreign arms deliveries, but parliamentary security figures have already called for urgent briefings on border interdiction and port defenses.
[79] Zo and Curro’s Asia Trip: Beijing Meetings and Investment Diplomacy
[80] ANCHOR:
 In a separate development, Palace sources and economic ministry officials confirm that King Zo III and Finance Minister Emilio Curro have returned from a high-profile diplomatic and business trip through Asia.
[81] The delegation spent roughly a week in Beijing, where they were seen meeting with senior Chinese officials and attending cultural events as part of what was described as a “strategic economic and cultural exchange.” RBD can confirm that one of the most prominent contacts during the visit included a meeting with Jiang Zemin, China’s top leader.
[82] During the Beijing leg, the Palace also confirmed that a stake in the Crown-linked energy venture Royla was sold to Chinese businessman Shao Luxian, a long-standing contact of both Zo and Curro. While exact contract terms were not released, sources describe the sale as bringing in several billion U.S. dollars and positioning Royla as a better-capitalized participant in Reunion’s emerging offshore oil program.
[83] Following Beijing, Zo and Curro continued their trip with stops in Japan and South Korea, meeting business and industrial figures, with discussions reportedly focused on port logistics, industrial tooling, electronics, and long-term investment relationships. On the return journey, they made a brief stop in Saudi Arabia, where Palace sources say they held meetings with members of the royal family regarding economic ties and regional stability.

 These developments come as Parliament faces mounting pressure to address economic anxiety at home while navigating growing international scrutiny abroad. RBD will continue to provide verified updates as official statements and diplomatic moves become public.
