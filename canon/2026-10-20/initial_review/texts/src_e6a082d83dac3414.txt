[1] Odds the market snaps and you slide into recession
[2] Given your mix right now (active war pressure, brittle confidence, a big foreign loan with a lockbox, import dependence, uneven industry, shaky logistics, and Parliament publicly flailing), the odds are ugly.
[3] Define “market crash” in Reunion terms
[4] Not a Wall Street-style crash — you don’t have that kind of market depth. A “crash” for you looks like:
[5] a currency confidence event (RMC sells off, spreads widen, importers demand USD terms),
[6] a short-term credit freeze (contractors won’t extend terms; banks tighten lending),
[7] and/or runs/hoarding behavior (fuel, staples, hard currency).

[16] Why the odds are so high
[17] War risk dominates pricing. If the ceasefire ends Sept 14 at 23:06 and fighting resumes, confidence breaks fast.
[18] The lockbox clause scares everyone. People hear “customs revenues are spoken for” and assume austerity or payment delays.
[19] Backwards/uneven industry + import dependence means any FX stress becomes real shortages quickly.
[20] Parliamentary paralysis is a multiplier: markets can tolerate bad news; they can’t tolerate “nobody is steering.”
[21] What lowers the crash odds fast
[22] A credible budget freeze/triage within 48 hours (not slogans).
[23] A public statement from Curro + Central Bank guaranteeing liquidity and explaining tranches plainly.
[24] Visible anti-hoarding actions without looking like martial law economics (targeted distribution, not blanket crackdowns).
[25] A clear plan for the ceasefire end (either extension track or defensive posture + logistics plan). Markets hate uncertainty more than bad outcomes.
