II. ROMAN–MEDIEVAL ERA
Greek Colonization (Pre-Roman)

Minor Greek colonies established trade outposts.

They did not deeply integrate inland.
They controlled ports and traded metals and timber.

Greek influence:

Maritime commerce culture
Early democratic councils in port towns
Coinage systems

But they never politically unified the island.

Roman Conquest (Early Empire)

Romans absorbed the territory after taking it from fragmented Greek holdings.

What Rome did:

Built road networks (still traceable today)
Constructed fortified hill towns
Integrated island into grain + naval system
Established legal Latin foundation
Created urban municipal culture

Romanization was deep.

Language:
Latin-based dialect roots remain in legal and administrative terms.

Infrastructure:
Stone roads and aqueduct ruins still form backbone of certain corridors.

Reunion identity myth later formed around:
“We are Roman before we are Balkan.”

Byzantine Control (Post-Western Collapse)

After Western Rome fell:
Byzantines took control.

They ruled indirectly.
Heavy taxation.
Military governors.

Cultural shift:
Greek liturgical influence.
Orthodox Christianity influence.

But resentment grew.

The Revolt — Battle of Reunion’s Peak

Local noble leader Discorius unified hill clans.

Byzantine garrison defeated in mountain engagement.

Key structural moment:

This is where the Kingdom of Disco emerges.

Independent monarchy forms.
Strong anti-imperial identity.

Founding myth:
“We bow to no empire.”

III. MEDIEVAL TO EARLY MODERN
Ottoman Expansion Period

Ottomans attempted maritime dominance.

Reunion resisted repeatedly.

Reasons they survived:

Strong navy relative to size.
Mountain interior defensive lines.
Ottoman focus prioritized mainland Balkans.

Reunion wins multiple naval engagements.
Avoids tribute status.

This period creates:
Militarized maritime identity.
Permanent suspicion of eastern empires.

Rise as Trade Power

Competes directly with Venice.

Reunion merchants:

Venetians decisively defeat Reunion fleet.

Reunion loses:

Major islands
Overseas holdings
Naval dominance

Turning point.

Reunion shifts from expansionist maritime power to defensive mercantile state.
